# Your Follow-Up Email Playbook
### Make every quote email feel personal, specific, and easy to say yes to.

Venus — your follow-ups are polite and your grammar is great. The one thing to fix: right now they read like they *could've been sent by any company to anyone.* The customer should feel like **you wrote it for them**, and there should be **one obvious next step.** Same idea as the calls — personal + specific, and make it easy to reply.

---

## The 5 fixes
1. **Name them + one human line.** Not "Hi," → "Hi Temo,". Reference their business, event, or past order.
2. **Name the product, not "the project."** "Your custom 10x10 tent setup," not "your project."
3. **Lead with our two superpowers: the free proof + 5-day delivery.** These are the *reasons* to say yes — and they were missing from every email. The free proof removes all risk ("see it with your logo before you pay a cent").
4. **One clear next step.** Replace "let me know if you have questions" with an easy yes: *"Reply YES and I'll send your free proof."*
5. **Warm, on-brand voice — and Spanish when they prefer it.** Cut the filler ("we truly appreciate the opportunity," "we value the opportunity to earn your business"). Sound like a real person who's got them.

## The structure (keep it to ~5 short lines)
1. Warm, personal opener (name + a human touch).
2. The quote + **what it includes** (name the product).
3. **Free proof + 5-day delivery.**
4. **One easy CTA.**
5. Signature.

Here's the easy part: I'll send a **free proof** first so you can see it with your logo before anything prints, and once you approve, we deliver in **5 business days**. Want me to start that proof? Just reply **YES**. — Venus

**Subject:** Still happy to send your free proof
> Hi **[Name]**, just floating this back up — no pressure at all. Any questions on the **[product]** quote ($[amount])? Happy to tweak anything. If it's easier, I can send the **free proof** so you can *see* it first — want me to? — Venus

### 3) Deadline nudge (Day 7 — event coming up)
**Subject:** Let's lock your [date] timing
> Hi **[Name]**, your **[event/date]** is getting close — to make sure you're covered, we'd want to start the **5-day production clock** soon. Want me to send the free proof today so we stay on schedule? Quick **YES** and I've got you. — Venus

## Before → After (one of your real emails)
**Before (the $888 one — generic, no product, no proof, passive close):**
> "I wanted to follow up and resend the quote… The total for the project is $888.00. We truly appreciate the opportunity… please feel free to reach out. Our team is always happy to help."

**After (specific + warm + free proof + one CTA):**
> "Hi [Name], Venus at Promo & Grow (formerly PrintCo Direct), following up on your **10ft SEG display** — **$888** all in. I'll send a **free proof** so you can see it with your logo before we print, then **5 business days** to delivery. Happy to adjust anything. Want me to get the proof started?"

See the difference? Shorter, names the product, gives the reasons to say yes, ends with one easy question.

## Using AI the right way (you've got Claude/ChatGPT)
The AI is only as good as what you feed it. Give it: **the client's name, the exact product, the amount, their event/date, and what they ordered before.** Tell it to use **this structure** and our warm voice — and reject anything that sounds like corporate filler. Paste prior-conversation screenshots so it has full context. The template above is the recipe; the AI just speeds it up.
