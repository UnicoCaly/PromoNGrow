## Post-delivery ask sent 2026-09-24 — and the reason to automate it

Tent and table cover **delivered 9/23 in two boxes**. Follow-up sent 9/24 asking three things: **did it arrive and how does the setup look**, **photos we can share on social**, and a **Google review** (`https://g.page/r/CV_K7jgJCVkPEAE/review`).

**This is the first deliberate post-order ask, and Carlos wants it automated for P&G going forward.** The case for it is straightforward: a custom branded tent is photographed by the customer anyway, at an event, in use — which is the single hardest asset for a print shop to produce and the most persuasive one to show. **Right now that asset exists on the customer's phone and never reaches us because nobody asks.**

**Design notes for the automation, before it gets built:**
- **Trigger on delivery, not on payment or fulfillment.** The gap between "shipped" and "in their hands" is where a premature ask lands wrong.
- **Ask for the photo before the review.** The photo is a favor and easy to say yes to; the review is a favor that costs more. The first makes the second likelier.
- **One follow-up, then stop.** A review sequence that nags costs more goodwill than the review is worth.
- **Do not automate it until it has been sent by hand a few more times** — Marisol is sample size one, and the wording is not yet proven.

**She also asked on 9/23: *"Do you have any pictures of what it looks like?"*** — which is the same asset problem seen from the buying side. **A customer asked for exactly what we are now asking customers to give us.** Every photo collected here closes that loop for the next buyer.
