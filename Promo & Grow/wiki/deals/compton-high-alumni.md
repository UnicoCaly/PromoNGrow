## Why it was lost — and it probably was not price

**Confirmed lost 2026-09-19: she went with a different vendor.** No post-mortem from her; what follows is
read off the record.

**The likeliest cause is a spec gap that was raised as a question instead of resolved.** She asked for an
**18″ valance**. We offered **17″**, because that is what we run — and the covering note asked *her* to
confirm she was comparing like for like against the other bids. **That put the comparison work on the
customer.** If a competitor quoted a true 18″, this was a specification loss and the question we asked her
was the moment it slipped.

**Price was not the lever, and the numbers prove it.** `#D158` already carried an effective **16.9%**
concession — 8% nonprofit plus $142.88 of sales tax absorbed inside the discount. Then `#D161` took a
**further 8% off a $1,500/tent price that already included a 10% discount** (Carlos, 9/19) — a double
discount nobody intended. **Total concession was past 20% and the job still went elsewhere.**

**What never arrived, and should have been read as a signal.** The **vector logo** — the gating input for
any production, due 9/16 — and the **501(c)(3) determination letter** that both discounts were conditional
on. She sent an AI mockup as reference only. Neither ever came. A buyer who is proceeding sends artwork; a
buyer who is still shopping sends questions.

**Also on the record:** a competitive bid against at least three quotes, a board deciding rather than her
alone, and a hard October 3 event date — all three stated by her in writing, all three pointing at a
process we were one input inside rather than leading.

**The two live Shopify drafts are deliberately not deleted.** `#D158` and `#D161` remain INVOICE_SENT and
technically payable. The pricing history is now evidence. Remove them only on Carlos's say-so.

**Policy consequences, both filed to [[master-price-sheet]] on 9/19:** nonprofits now **pay sales tax** —
the absorb-into-the-discount structure invented for this account is retired; the 8% nonprofit rate survives
for organizations with a great cause. And a new **10% volume discount on $1,000+ multi-piece orders when
asked**, which came out of the Firm Footing quote the same week.

## The discount and the tax — the two invoices do not match

**Read this before quoting the next nonprofit.** #D158 and #D161 are structured two different ways, and
only one of them is the house rule.

**#D158 is correct and always was.** Live in Shopify right now: tax-exempt flag **off**, line items
taxable, order discount **$270.88 as a fixed amount** titled *"Compton High Alumni — nonprofit rate
(8% + sales tax covered)"*, taxable subtotal $1,329.12, sales tax $142.88, total $1,472.00. That is the
8% nonprofit discount ($128) **plus the $142.88 of sales tax absorbed inside the discount line** rather
than waived — an effective concession of **16.9%**. It is exactly what [[master-price-sheet]] says to
do, and it is what Carlos ruled on 9/10.

The sequence matters, because it is what caused the confusion: the invoice **email** went out at
5:31 PM on 9/10 with the original 8% / $0-tax structure, and Carlos restructured the draft about two
and a half hours later. Shopify invoice links are live, so Deborah's link shows the corrected
breakdown at the same $1,472 total and nothing needed walking back with her. **The email text
describes a structure that no longer exists.** Anyone reading that email as the source of truth will
get this wrong.

**#D161 was built wrong — by reading that email instead of the live invoice.** It went out on 9/16
with the tax-exempt flag **on**, line items non-taxable, and a straight 8% percentage discount: $3,000
− $240 = $2,760, $0 tax. That puts **"tax exempt" on a P&G order, which the price sheet says never
happens**, and claims an exemption on a 501(c)(3) whose determination letter has not arrived.

**The fix, if Carlos wants it applied:** hold her out-the-door at **$2,760** and mirror #D158 —
tax-exempt off, line items taxable, fixed discount **$507.90**, giving $3,000 − $507.90 = $2,492.10
taxable + $267.90 tax @ 10.75% = **$2,760.00**. Effective concession 16.9%, the same as #D158. Her
price does not move; only the structure does, and the precedent for changing it after sending is
#D158 itself.

**Left as-sent for now.** An attempt to apply this on 9/16 dropped the total to $2,492.10 because the
tax would not compute on the draft, and it was reverted to the as-sent $2,760 within the minute. The
tax rate needs to resolve off the address on the draft before this is retried — that is the piece to
check first.

**Both estimates depend on the 501(c)(3), and the determination letter and EIN have still not
arrived.** #D158's discount line says *"sales tax covered"* and #D161's says *"501(c)(3) —
determination letter to be provided"* to a customer whose paperwork is not on file. Close that before
