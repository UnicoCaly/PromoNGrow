# Outbound Method — the Value Statement Framework

**Summary**: A unified cold-outreach methodology (Conor/Connor Murray — ex-#1 SDR at Oracle, now Enterprise AE; Higher Levels / Cold Email Engine) distilled from three 2026-06-19/20 training sources. Cold call and cold email run on the **same spine**: deliver value first, answer who/why/what in seconds, use assumptive (not passive) language, batch the prep ("coil the spring"), follow up tight, and track three inputs. Also covers the email-specific depth — persona levels, subject lines, the follow-up ladder, objection-over-email, and calendar/confirmation. Built for B2B-SaaS SDRs booking meetings — this page adapts it to PNG, where **Venus is the closer and the "meeting" = the free-proof / quote conversation.**

**Type**: concept

**Sources**: "The Ultimate Guide to Cold Calling in 2026.pdf" (Conor Murray — "Cold Call Mastery"); " 10 Years of Expert Cold Email Advice in 36 Minutes (B2B Sales) - Connor Murray.pdf" (Higher Levels interview); " Cold Emailing in 2026: The Only System SDRs and AEs Need.pdf" (the full email blueprint).

## The core spine (applies to call AND email)

1. **Value Statement Framework** — answer three questions, in ~30–45 seconds on a call or 3 short paragraphs in an email: **WHO** you are (one line), **WHY** you're reaching out (the *value* — the priorities / pains you solve that are relevant to them, plus a high-level *how*), **WHAT** you want (the next step — time on the calendar). The burden of proof is on the caller to deliver value *first*, before asking for anything.
2. **Assumptive, not passive language (the #1 fix).** Kill "if you're interested / is this worth a chat / worth exploring / I was hoping to set up some time." Replace with assumptive: *"I'm looking to set some time — does Wednesday or Thursday work?"* The underlying assumption is that the next step is happening; you're not asking permission for it.
3. **Tonality is ~90% of success.** Use a **downward inflection** (signals "I have something to say, I'm not here to waste your time"); an upward inflection signals you'll be a while and invites a brush-off. A good-enough script + the right tone beats a perfect script delivered nervously. Rehearse scripts ~10 min/day so they're muscle memory and sound natural, not read.
4. **Coil the spring (do the heavy lifting UPFRONT).** Before you ever dial/send, build **homogeneous lists** by buying trigger (job title / department / industry / segment) and write a **template per segment**. Then daily execution is just "pick a list, pick a script, go" — personalization happens at the *list* level, not per message. This is what takes a rep from 20 to 100 touches/day *with less effort*.
5. **Sell the next step, not the whole solution.** On a cold touch you're selling the *meeting* (the time), not the entire value prop. Gives you multiple "shots on net." (For PNG: the next step is the **free proof / quote**, not a 30-min meeting — see the adaptation below.)
6. **Keep an objection response bank.** Base templates for the recurring objections ("no budget," "already have a solution," "not a priority"), plus prospect-specific context bolted on. First move on any objection = **"double down and sell the next step"**: acknowledge the concern, *"makes sense to me anyway,"* and keep it pointed at the meeting/proof. Then 2–3 **targeted credibility questions** to earn the right to open the conversation.
7. **Track three inputs.** Every "no" is a data point; volume rides out the variance in answer/reply rates. Calls: dials → answers → meetings. Email: open rate → reply rate → meeting-booked rate. Each diagnoses a *different* problem (see Tracking below).

## Cold call specifics

- **Does it still work? Yes — better than ever, *because* almost no one does it.** Estimate ~100 cold emails sent per 1 cold call; calls cut through. Cold calling is hard to automate → a durable, defensible skill.
- **Opener = "assumptive formality."** *"Hey [name], this is [you] from [company], how are you?"* — delivered with a **downward** inflection — then go straight into the value statement. Don't confirm their name/title (triggers fight-or-flight: "who is this?"). Don't ask permission ("got 27 seconds?" with an upward lilt signals you'll be a while).
- **30-second script:** WHO (one line) → WHY (the value: relevant priorities/pains + high-level how) → WHAT (the next step / time). Bold the swappable parts so one script structure serves a whole persona list.
- **Volume / optimizing:** structure the day so you're *not* researching between calls (no pulling up LinkedIn each time) — that friction is why most reps stall at 25–40 dials. The goal is "just dial." 1–3 min per dial. Volume also accelerates skill and rides out cold streaks.
- **Objection handling:** (1) **double down and sell the time** — prospects decline the first meeting ask even when interested; (2) ask **2–3 targeted credibility questions** ("when you say you already use X — does it handle Y? The reason I ask is, when we work with X customers there's usually a gap in Z…"); if they're still on the line, you've earned the right to open up the conversation.

## Cold email specifics

- **Avoid both traps.** Sales-enablement emails = too long (5 paragraphs), over-complicated, fluffy 15-touch cadences. LinkedIn-guru emails = too short/cutesy, fake personalization ("fellow eagle / both dog dads") that reads as manipulative and doesn't scale. Both are too soft and not direct. Go **direct**: who you are, why you're reaching out, what you want — centered on the next step.
- **Structure:** 4–6 sentences, **3 short paragraphs** (who / why / what — the brain likes threes), fits on one phone screen. Assumptive CTA = a specific time / next step, never "is this worth a chat?"
- **Cadence — 70–80% of meetings come from FOLLOW-UPS, not email #1.** The first email casts the hook; the follow-ups land the meeting by redirecting attention back to it.
  - Initial + **3 follow-ups (maybe 4)**, **every other day (24–48h apart** — past 48h reply rates drop sharply), ~7–8 day span.
  - Don't run obvious automation ("a new email every Monday" is transparent). Don't do 15–20-touch cadences — after ~4–5 emails you devalue yourself; move to a **fresh list** instead.
  - **FU1 — benefit of the doubt:** *"Just want to make sure you caught my note — do either of those dates work, or let me know if you have time this week?"*
  - **FU2 — the single highest-booking line:** *"Please give me your thoughts on this."* (Seen in preview → they scroll down and finally read email #1.)
  - **FU3 — assumptive break-up:** *"Is next month a better time to talk about this? Just want to close the loop either way — let me know."*
  - **FU4 — last resort, name only:** your first name on its own line (*"[name],"*) — a soft final nudge.
  - Every follow-up is short and points them back to the first email. **Never more than ~2 business days between touches.** After ~5 emails (1 + 4) over 7–10 days, **stop and reset to a fresh list** — reply rates fall off a cliff and dragging on devalues you. **Return to a non-responsive list in 6–8 weeks** (it's often bad timing, not a real no).
- **ABAB rhythm:** list A Monday, list B Tuesday, follow-up A Wednesday, follow-up B Thursday… an every-other-day cadence that takes ~1 hour each morning, freeing the rest of the day for calls. Ideally alternate channels: email Mon / call Tue / email Wed / call Thu.
- **Two buckets:** (a) **high-volume** — repeatable per-segment templates, going for replies (any reply — yes/no/objection — beats silence); (b) **strategic accounts** — a small weekly set you research (10-K / earnings / news), worked multi-channel (phone + email + LinkedIn). Best strategic opener: *"I spoke with [name] and understand [X] is a big focus for you this year"* — signals you did the homework, so everything after carries weight.

## Persona levels — write to who actually reads it

Adapt the **WHY** to where the reader sits; priorities differ by level (same who/why/what structure, different *why*):
- **Level 1 — end users / ground floor.** Care about saving time, reducing friction, executing day-to-day (the people *in* the tools).
- **Level 2 — middle management.** Own team KPIs + process; care about efficiency, reporting, team performance.
- **Level 3 — executives / budget holders (VP+).** Care about ROI, risk reduction, strategic alignment; rarely in the tools. Simplify to "above the line / below the line" if three is too many.

**PNG adaptation:** PNG's targets are owner-led SMBs (1–50 employees), so the three levels usually **collapse into one person** — the owner is the end-user *and* the budget-holder. The relevant axis for PNG isn't org rank, it's **business type / use-case** (restaurant vs event planner vs realtor), already mapped in the [[cold-email-sequence]] `{{product_angle}}` library and [[sell-guide]].

## Personalize without breaking scale

You don't have to choose between a spray-and-pray template and 30-minutes-per-email research. Layer **one** context line — a real observation about their company/role — on top of the repeatable who/why/what. Best version = **contextual outreach**: reference something real ("I spoke with [name] and understand [X] is a focus this year"), then the same structure. Keep the research-heavy bucket small (you can't do it for 100/day); the high-volume bucket runs the clean template.

## Subject lines — clarity beats cleverness

The subject line's job is **not to win — it's to not lose the email.** Overthinking it gets you flagged or ignored.
- **Avoid** salesy/markety lines ("Let's 10x your [thing]", "improve your ops by 32%", "Can you help me?") — they read fake and trip spam filters.
- **Use** plain, what-it's-about, **3–4 words max**: `intro` / `introduction` · their name / your name · their company / your company · `[topic] for [company]`.
- Win with the *email*, not the subject. **PNG:** keep the sequence's lowercase, human subjects ("quick idea for {{business}}", "una idea para {{business}}", "intro — Promo & Grow"); a genuine free offer ("free mockup with your logo") is fine because it's real, not hype — anything that reads like a discount blast is not.

## Objection handling over email

When a reply is an objection ("we already have a vendor / no budget / not the right person"), **do not argue the objection in the thread** — those emails don't get replies. Instead: acknowledge → *"makes sense to connect anyway"* → **double down and sell the next step** (the call / the free proof), not why you're the better choice. Keep an **objection bank by category** so you're not writing from scratch each time. Selling the next step is a far easier "yes" than selling the switch. *(PNG's bank lives in [[deal-followup-playbook]].)*

## Calendar invites / the confirmation — protect the show-rate

The most overlooked step: you did the work to book it — now make sure they show. A blank or fluffy invite ("thank you so much!! 🙏 learn about your role") gets cancelled Friday morning. Give them something to **bite off on**: a clear attendees list + a short proposed agenda (current state → their challenge → how we work with similar orgs → example outcome → next step) so days later they remember what it's even about.

**PNG adaptation:** PNG rarely books a 30-min calendar meeting — the next step is the **free proof / quote**. The equivalent is the **confirmation message**: never a blank "got it." Restate the product, "free proof first," the *5 business days from approval* timeline, and the one next action ("send your logo and I'll start the proof today"). Concrete beats fluffy, and it protects the close.

## Tracking (the three inputs diagnose different problems)

**Email is a scale game — tiny input gains compound.** Example: 150 emails/wk × 4% reply × 20% reply→meeting ≈ 1.2 meetings/wk. Nudge it to 250 emails + 5% reply + 22% conversion and it's ~2.75/wk — a **~150% jump** from changes you'd never feel day-to-day. **Responses are the #1 driver** (the email equivalent of answer-rate on calls — no reply, no shot on net), so write language/tone that *triggers a reply*, especially around the ask. That's why the three inputs below are worth obsessing over:

- **Low open rate** → subject line, the opening text, or follow-up velocity.
- **Low reply rate** (opens are fine) → passive language / weak CTA; tighten to assumptive.
- **Low meeting-booked rate** (replies are fine) → the *why* is off; sharpen the priorities/challenges named and the how-we-solve-it.
- 1–2% gains on each compound hard across a quarter and across a team.

## How PNG / Venus applies this (the adaptation)

The method is built for SaaS SDRs booking a meeting for an AE. PNG is more transactional and **Venus is the closer**, so the spine transfers but the *next step* changes:

- **The "meeting / time" → PNG's next step = the free proof / the quote conversation.** Venus's assumptive CTA becomes *"I'll get a free proof started — send me your logo and the quantity you're thinking"* or *"want me to put a quick quote together?"* — specific, assumptive, never "would you maybe be interested?"
- **Coil the spring → already done for her.** Her lists are pre-batched: the [[reactivation-call-queue]] cohorts, the [[venus-work-queue]] tiers, the [[apollo-net-new-list]] segments. Scripts-per-segment live in [[sell-guide]] (cross-sell angles) and [[cold-email-segment-copy]].
- **Assumptive + downward inflection → rewrite the passive lines** in her openers and follow-ups (a pass on [[venus-sales-playbook]] + [[deal-followup-playbook]]).
- **Cadence → maps onto the existing Day-1/3/7 chase** ([[draft-order-quote-system]] / [[deal-followup-playbook]]). Tighten unpaid-quote follow-ups toward 24–48h and add the *"please give me your thoughts on this"* + assumptive-break-up lines.
- **Objection bank → fold PNG's real objections** (price, "just looking," "send me info," "I already have a printer") into [[deal-followup-playbook]] as base responses, each ending in the free-proof next step.
- **Scale honesty:** Venus is one closer, 10am–4pm PT, running a concierge close — apply the **structure** (batched lists, ready scripts, tight cadence, objection bank, tracked inputs), not the literal 100-dials/day SDR number. The volume figures are directional, not a quota.
- **Track** dials / answers / quotes-sent / proofs / closes in the Sheet — ties to [[operating-rhythm]] and [[lead-tracker-sheet]].
