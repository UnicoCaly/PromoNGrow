# Getting Customers Fast — the lead-gen principles

**Summary**: Distilled from Alex Hormozi's "How to get customers so fast it feels illegal" — ten lead-generation principles, each mapped to where Promo & Grow already lives it, should push it, or has a gap. This is the **customer-acquisition** counterpart to [[email-campaign-engine]] (which distills Hormozi's *warm-list* model); together they cover get-the-lead and monetize-the-list. The through-line that matters most for P&G right now: **most of these principles are trust mechanisms** — free value, minimizing "need-to-believes," and truth-first marketing are three angles on the exact **trust gap** Venus is hearing from leads ([[venus-social-ads-playbook]]). Hormozi's dollar figures come from a $250M info-product portfolio — **the principles transfer; the numbers and the portfolio-scale moves do not.**

**Type**: concept

**Sources**: "Alex Hormozi - How to get customers so fast it feels illegal.pdf" (YouTube transcript, ingested 2026-07-14).

**Last updated**: 2026-07-14.

## 1. Give away for free what others charge for (and make it better)

His #1 lead strategy: find what competitors *charge* for, compute your hard cost to deliver it, give that away free, then measure leads → conversions. Give away something **significantly better** than everyone else — ideally the thing they charge for. It's build-once, get-unlimited-leads.

**P&G is already doing this — it's the free proof.** Competitors charge for mockups/proofs or don't offer them at all (verified whitespace in [[meta-creative-expansion-2026-07]]); P&G places your art on the product and shows it **before you pay**. The hard cost is Venus's proof time; the return is the whole [[conversion-bridge]]. **The push:** treat the free proof as the *headline* lead magnet, not a footnote — and keep it honestly scoped to **$500+ big-ticket** ([[master-price-sheet]] ruling), because that's where the value is real. This principle *validates* the free-proof play; it doesn't ask us to invent anything.

## 2. Fewer, better (marketing talent)

The best advertisers don't need many people. A "10" marketer with an iPhone can out-produce a department of "2s" by orders of magnitude. Hormozi cut gym-launch marketing 17→5 and **tripled output**. Keep the marketing team tight, put the highest-leverage person in the highest-leverage seat, and — critically — **the founder / best marketer must stay close to the marketing**, not manage it from a distance where skill gets diluted.

**P&G maps to this cleanly and it's a reason not to over-hire.** The whole operating model is deliberately lean: Carlos on the close, Venus as the high-leverage on-camera bilingual host + ads owner ([[venus-social-ads-playbook]]), Claude-admin + Claude Code as force multipliers ([[claude-code]]). The lesson: resist adding "bodies" to social/ads; bet on Venus as the "10" and keep Carlos close to the creative direction, not walled off from it. (Same meta-lesson as [[email-campaign-engine]]'s "trust smart people, make bets.")

## 3. "What would it take to be #1?"

The highest-leverage question. It circumvents incremental 10%-better thinking by assuming the goal outcome and reverse-engineering to the present. Usually one or two big things would have to be true — and more often than not you already have the resources, you just haven't pointed them at it. Schwarzman's corollary: **thinking big is about as hard as thinking small**, so aim at the bigger prize with the same effort. Resourcefulness > resources.

**P&G translation:** the honest version of "#1" here is #1 in the **Lynwood / LA bilingual custom-event-display niche** (see "sell in a vacuum" below), not #1 nationally against BuildASign. Worth running the exercise explicitly at a weekly sync: *what would have to be true for P&G to own the LA bilingual event-vendor market?* — and checking which of those things the current resources (Venus, the ad account, the real-install library, the 20-yr heritage) could already start on. Feeds [[the-20k-math]] and [[growth-offensive-v2]].

## 4. Sell in a vacuum (no price competition)

Two ways to escape competing on price: **sell where no one else sells** (the empty pond), or **sell what no one else sells** (a feature/service others can't match → command a premium). Either way the customer is choosing you-or-nothing, not you-vs-six-others.

**This is P&G's positioning thesis, already mapped.** The vacuum = **bilingual + local + free-proof + 5-day + real installs** — the competitor whitespace verified in [[meta-creative-expansion-2026-07]] and [[competitive-war-room]] (door-to-door honesty · proof fidelity · transparent pricing · bilingual local). The national printers can't do the bilingual-local-concierge thing; the local shops don't do the proof-fidelity + speed. **The push:** in ad copy and Venus's content, lean *harder* into the you-or-nothing framing rather than any price message — premium-not-cheap ([[png-voice]]).

## 5. An ounce of pre is worth a pound of post

The leverage in content is in **pre-production** — the idea, the hook, the concept — not the editing. A video *concept* more people want to click will out-perform a poorly-executed better idea by an order of magnitude. Most businesses do **education** marketing, and for education the job of post-production is only to **aid comprehension** (make it simple), not to add razzle-dazzle. Strip the post; move the work forward into "is this interesting? can I make it simple?" (Calculus/Physics Wallah: one guy, a whiteboard, 10–20M views.)

**This is directly actionable for Venus's content production** ([[venus-social-ads-playbook]] / [[venus-content-calendar-2026-07]]). It says: spend the time up front on the **hook and the point of each post** — not on CapCut effects. For P&G's education-heavy pillars (size guides, "what a custom 10×10 actually costs," proof-vs-delivered), the win is a clear idea simply shown, shot against the real shop floor. It also lowers the production bar, which protects Venus's cadence. Pairs with the hook craft in [[copywriting-method]].

## 6. Minimize the "need-to-believes"

The fewer things a customer must **believe or learn** to buy, the faster they buy. Repackage the offer so there are **zero or one** beliefs required. Amazon/Uber succeed because they took the belief down to almost half-a-belief ("someone might buy my thing"; "my phone dings, I get paid"). Branding, at scale, is conquering need-to-believes *before* the sale. And **giving away free stuff moves a prospect from "needs more information" to "needs less."**

**This is the trust gap, named in Hormozi's language.** A cold lead handing a stranger $800 has too many need-to-believes: *will it look right? will it arrive on time? are these people real? will they take my money and vanish?* Every P&G trust asset is a belief-remover: the **free proof** kills "will it look right" (you see it first); the **honest 5-day-from-approval SLA** kills "will it arrive"; **real installs + faces + reviews + 20-yr heritage** ([[venus-social-ads-playbook]]'s content pillars) kill "are they real." **The frame to use:** for any new offer or ad, list the beliefs required and design them down to one. This is the strategic spine connecting the free proof, the content plan, and the ads.

## 7. Split-test in leverage order

When you can't get an order-of-magnitude jump, stack 10–30% lifts across several points in the funnel — double or triple total output, and profit rises *faster* than revenue (the new revenue rides on the same fixed cost). Test in this order of leverage: **(1) the offer** (the thing you give away) → **(2) the packaging** (headlines + sub-headlines) → **(3) the images** → **(4) third-party integrations** (the sleeper — swapping the tool that captures the lead/checkout, because no vendor reports *their* conversion rate). Each win is one-time effort for a permanent lift.

**For the ads + landing funnel Venus manages** ([[venus-social-ads-playbook]], [[meta-creative-expansion-2026-07]], [[free-proof-landing-page]]): test the **offer and the headline before the image** — the opposite of most people's instinct to fiddle with visuals first. #4 is a real P&G lever too: the lead **form** itself is a third-party integration, and the 10.3% logo-collection leak (form 2.0 fix, [[meta-lead-intake-playbook]]) is exactly the kind of capture-tool conversion problem Hormozi is pointing at.

## 8. Be able to spend more — LTV is the arms race

Businesses say "leads cost too much"; the real problem is usually **they don't make enough per customer.** Cost-to-acquire is roughly similar across competitors in a category (benchmark it: *"average cost per customer [industry]"*); the difference between winners and losers is **LTV** — Starbucks's ~$14K LTV lets it outspend the local shop even while being less efficient. Ads only get **more expensive over time**, so you need LTV rising concurrently to keep reaching colder, pricier audiences at a profit. The strongest LTV lever is the scariest one owners avoid: **raising prices**. And the first campaign in a new market/offer always performs best — build the business to accommodate that decay, don't chase the first cheap-lead high.

**P&G's honest read:** the constraint has been repeatedly measured as **close-capacity, not lead cost** ([[meta-funnel-review-2026-07-09]], [[meta-ugc-launch-kit]]) — CPL is healthy ($4.90). So the Hormozi lever that applies most is the **LTV side**: reorders + reactivation ([[reactivation-outreach]], [[email-campaign-engine]]) and the **premium-not-cheap** posture that protects price ([[pricing-engine]], [[master-price-sheet]] — note the 10%-in-trade margin is deliberately *capped* so discounting doesn't erode LTV). The caution flag: don't answer a future lead-cost complaint by hunting cheaper leads; answer it by raising per-customer value. Feeds [[the-20k-math]] (the AOV lever) and [[concierge-close]].

## 9. Affiliates "so good it hurts" — and why 10% doesn't move anyone

A fast way to borrow an audience someone else spent years building: get promoted in front of it. But normal affiliate deals fail because **nobody burns their hard-won trust to hand you a customer for 20%.** The fix: **peel a corner off your product and give the affiliate 100% of that piece's revenue.** His masseuse example: let a partner business sell "3 massages for $500+" and keep *all* of it; your cost is ~$90 of labor for a **pre-paid, pre-qualified** lead who shows up on your calendar — a ~$270 CAC for a $2,000 customer, with zero ad/landing/chase work. The behavior-change threshold is blunt: **incentives under ~50% don't change behavior** ("10% off — who cares; either I buy the jacket or I don't").

**⚠️ Tension to flag for Carlos — read against the 2026-07-13 ruling.** P&G just ratified a **10%-in-trade** closer's margin that includes **referral** as one of the earn-able commitments ([[master-price-sheet]]). Hormozi's "10% doesn't move behavior" is aimed at exactly this kind of *referral/affiliate* incentive. **The two aren't a flat contradiction** — the 10%-in-trade is a *closer's concession inside a live negotiation* (a lever Venus trades for a deposit/pickup/review/referral to close *today*), which is a different mechanism than a standing bounty paid to a third party to *send* customers. But the **referral leg specifically** is the weak one by Hormozi's logic: 10% off a future order is unlikely to make a happy customer or a partner business actively hustle referrals. **The opportunity:** if P&G wants a real referral/partner *engine* (vs. a passive discount), the peel-a-corner model is the stronger design — e.g., let an event-planner or party-rental partner sell a P&G "starter kit" and keep the margin, delivering P&G a pre-paid, pre-qualified tent/display lead. This is a **Carlos decision**, not a wiki change; noted here and on [[master-price-sheet]] as a design question, and it connects to the partnership net in [[outbound-fishing-playbook]] / [[growth-offensive-v2]].

## 10. Nothing sells like the truth

Truth should be your greatest ally, not your liability — lies get customers fast but burn reputation. "Authentic" just means **say what's true**, including the unflattering things, because they often help: a small business *is* scarcity (fewer slots) + personalization + "buy local" — pros, not cons, to the right buyer. The technique is the **damaging admission**: *"[bad], **but** [good]"* — "but" amplifies the good ("our parking's bad, **but** you'll have so much fun you won't care"). Claim your negatives first (Eminem in *8 Mile*) so no one can wield them against you. The hardest thing in all of marketing is **getting someone to believe you** — so every trust-increasing move is among the best ad investments you can make. Truth is the only long-term strategy.

**This is P&G's voice already** — [[png-voice]] is truth-first, anti-overpromise, honest-SLA ("5 days means 5 days," never "guaranteed"). What's *new to steal* is the **damaging-admission technique** as a copy move for the trust gap: P&G's honest "negatives" are actually its wedges — *"We're a 20-year Lynwood shop, not a national warehouse — **so** you get a real bilingual human who shows you the proof before you pay."* *"We don't do rush-in-a-day — **so** what you get in 5 days is actually right."* Owning the small-local-slower truths *converts* them into the premium-concierge story. Add this to the craft layer in [[copywriting-method]] and Venus's content/ad hooks ([[venus-social-ads-playbook]]).

## Honest caveat — what transfers and what doesn't

Hormozi teaches from a **$250M info-product portfolio** (high-ticket digital offers, huge lists, portfolio-level talent wars, national LTV optimization). Sort the ten before spending on any:

- **Transfers cleanly to P&G now:** #1 free value (already = free proof), #5 pre>post (Venus's content), #6 need-to-believes (the trust-gap spine), #7 split-test order (ads/landing), #10 truth/damaging-admission (voice + hooks). These are the ones to *act on*.
- **Transfers as a lens, not a spend:** #2 fewer-better (a hiring discipline), #3 what-would-#1-take (a planning exercise scoped to the *LA bilingual niche*, not the nation), #4 sell-in-a-vacuum (already the positioning), #8 LTV (relevant, but P&G's real constraint is close-capacity, not lead cost — apply it to reorders/pricing, not ad-budget escalation).
- **Aspirational / needs a Carlos decision:** #9 affiliate model — powerful, but a net-new program design and in tension with the just-ratified 10% referral leg; don't build without deciding.

The dollar figures ($90 CACs, $2K offers, $2.4M campaigns) are info-product economics — **do not quote them for P&G.** Sourced P&G numbers live on [[dashboard]] and [[the-20k-math]].
