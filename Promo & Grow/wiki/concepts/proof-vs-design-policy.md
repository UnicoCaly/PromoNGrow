# Proof vs. Design Policy

**Summary**: The line that keeps the free-proof offer profitable instead of turning into a free design shop. One rule governs it: **we place YOUR art on the product for free (the proof); we CREATE art for a fee (design).** Placement is a sales tool; creation is labor. A 3-tier ladder (free proof → file-fix/light design → custom/complex design) plus a revisions cap operationalizes it. **Dollar amounts confirmed by Carlos 2026-06-24** and filed as the Venus-facing [[design-price-rubric]]. **The free-proof half of this is under review as of 2026-09-24** — see the review section below; the leak is proofing before Deposit-to-Lock, not the revision count.

## The governing line: place vs. create

**We put YOUR art on the product for free. We CREATE art for a fee.**

- **Proof** = take ready artwork the customer already has and *place* it on the product (size, color-check, show it on the real tent/throw/flag) → a mockup. Free — it's the conversion hook ("see it before you pay").
- **Design** = *create* or substantially *build* artwork (make a logo, redraw unusable art, compose original/complex layouts, brand systems). Labor → charged.

Every edge case resolves to: *am I still placing their art, or am I now creating?*

## The 3-tier ladder

| Tier                            | What it is                                                                                                                       | Price                                                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1 — Free Proof** *(the hook)* | Customer sends print-ready art; we place it + send a mockup. Includes ~2 rounds of placement tweaks.                             | **Free** — on **$500+ big-ticket only** (tents, displays, bundles), per the 6/22 ruling. Small print (cards/flyers) excluded; design charged.                               |
| **2 — File Fix / Light Design** | File isn't usable as-is: vectorize a blurry logo, redraw, background cleanup beyond trivial, simple layout of supplied elements. | **Flat fee $50–$85** (vectorize $50 / detailed-logo $80 / light layout $70 / multi-piece $85). Credited to order only on **$500+** orders; on sub-$500 print the fee stays. |
| **3 — Custom / Complex Design** | We *create*: original artwork, logo design, multi-element/complex layouts, custom step-and-repeat, brand systems.                | **Flat tiers $95 / $175 / $295+ — charged, NEVER credited.** Quote before doing the work. Full brand / logo-creation / wraps → escalate to **Proágo**.                      |

Tier 1 is what the ads promise (assumes the customer HAS a logo). Tier 3 is the "I definitely need to charge" case. Tier 2 is the middle that usually gets fumbled into free work — name it, price it. **Set fees + the complexity-rating reference (how Venus lands the right tier) live on the [[design-price-rubric]].**

## Revisions before ordering

The trigger is **place vs. create**, not a round count:
- **Free:** moving/resizing/recoloring the customer's *supplied* art ("bigger," "match this red," "try it on black"). Soft guideline ~2 rounds — used as the moment to ask "*still placing, or now creating?*", not as a hard wall (keep it warm — *"let's nail it,"* not *"you're out of revisions"*).
- **Becomes paid the moment it's creation** (new elements, redrawing, "can you design something that…") → Tier 2/3, **quote the fee before doing the work**.
- **Post-approval = locked.** The approved proof is what prints ("nothing prints without your approval"); no revisions after the order.

## Guardrails (protect the offer)
1. Free proof = **placement only**, stated plainly.
2. Free proof = **$500+ items only** (the ruling) — a $50 flag doesn't earn free design.
3. **Quote design before doing it** — never deliver custom work then ask for money.
4. Warm framing, never bait-and-switch: *"The proof's always free — that's your logo on your tent. What you're describing is new artwork, which is design work — let me quote that for you."*

## How it runs (operational)

Venus triages every free-proof request against the [[file-prep-gate]]:
- Art ready → **free proof** (Tier 1).
- Art needs work → **quote the art fee** (Tier 2) from a design-price rubric.
- "Can you create / design…" → **Tier 3 quote**, or escalate complex/brand work to Proágo.

## UNDER REVIEW 2026-09-24 — the leak is proofing before money. Separately: proof delivery has no confirmation loop.

**Carlos, 2026-09-24:** *"I think i'm going to have to rethink the free proofing policy."* Not ruled. What follows is the evidence the ruling should answer to — **and one case that was first filed here in error and does not belong to this policy at all.**

### The actual policy failure: Titan Law

**Titan Law (`#D166`, $1,796.74).** Four revision rounds against a policy that allows about two, on an order where the **Deposit-to-Lock rule (50% on $800+) already existed and was simply not applied.** Nothing was collected until after round four.

**The revisions cap was never the binding constraint.** The money gate was, and it was skipped. Tightening the cap on top of an unapplied gate changes nothing. **Move the gate, do not tighten the cap:** on $800+, the free proof comes *after* the deposit. A paid customer revising four times is a served customer, not a leak. Two rounds before money, unlimited after, is enforceable and reads as generous rather than restrictive.

### CORRECTION — Dr. Silegy / Gloria is a delivery failure, not a policy failure

An earlier version of this section filed Gloria (`#P&G881079`, 9/24) as a case of the portal rule being *"waived the moment it became thirty seconds of friction."* **That was wrong, and it was wrong because it was inferred from one email exchange instead of the nine days behind it.**

**What actually happened, per Carlos:**
- The proof was sent through Shopify Proofer **four or five times, starting 9/15**.
- She was **not receiving or not seeing any of them**.
- Carlos **called twice the prior week** — no answer.
- By the 9/24 call she was **annoyed**, and still had nothing.
- He sent it from his own email instead and she approved in writing the same hour.

**That is not a concession. It is nine days of a dead channel and the correct call to abandon it.** Continuing to push a customer through a delivery path that had already failed five times would have been the mistake.

### The real finding: **there is no delivery confirmation on proofs**

**Gloria is the only recipient Proofer is failing.** Marisol (Evoria) and Darling (Titan) both received and approved online in the same window. So this is recipient-side, not a broken app.

**The cost is not the waiver — it is that nobody knew for nine days.** Five sends produced no signal that nothing was landing. The first real signal was a customer's irritation on a phone call. **An order sat one step from production for nine days because a send was assumed to be a delivery.**

**Standing rule (Carlos to confirm):** **two sends with no portal activity inside 48 hours ends the channel.** Switch to direct email with the proof attached, and call. Do not send a third time through the same path. Written email approval — *"I APPROVE THEM."* — is a valid approval of record; put that in the policy rather than granting it as an exception under pressure.

**Deliverability to chase, separately from this policy:** Proofer sends from **`order_proofs@shopfox.io`**, a third-party domain that is not `promongrow.com`. Gloria is at **`drsilegy.com`**, which runs Microsoft/Outlook. Microsoft tenant-level quarantine holds mail *without* dropping it in the user's Junk folder, which matches exactly what she reported — she could not find it in spam because it never reached her mailbox. **Worth checking whether Proofer's sending domain is authenticated (SPF/DKIM/DMARC) for our store, and whether a from-address on our own domain is configurable.** If a dental office on Microsoft silently eats our proofs, Gloria is not the last one.

**Maintain a known-bad-delivery list.** `drsilegy.com` goes on it today: send that account proofs by direct email from the start.

### The design test for whatever replaces free proofing

Not *"how many rounds."* It is: **does the gate still hold at the moment a real customer is mildly inconvenienced and the order is not yet paid?** Titan is the case that fails that test. Gloria is not — and a policy review that counts a delivery outage as a discipline problem will produce a rule aimed at the wrong thing.
