## Quoting rules (quick reference)
1. On the sheet → set price. 2. Custom → route-through to Carlos. 3. Minimum order **$100**. 4. **From 9/28: no free shipping, no ask-and-receive volume discount.** Discounts → **the 10% closer's margin, in-trade only** (deposit today / pickup / Google review / referral — never as an opener, never stacked); deeper or off-sheet → Carlos (see the closing-authority ruling below). 5. Complex branding (wraps, channel letters, brand systems) → pitch + escalate to Proágo. 6. Always close with **free proof ($500+ items) + 5-day delivery** (4-day print) — and from 9/28, **shipping quoted separately or pickup in Lynwood**. *Tu evento, tu marca, hecho bien.* (source: PNG-Master-Price-Sheet.md, SOP-01-Quoting-and-Pricing.md)

> **Charging for design?** When a job needs art *created* or *fixed* (not just placed), quote from the **[[design-price-rubric]]** — the design counterpart to this sheet (vectorize $50 · light layout $70 · custom $95/$175/$295+), with a complexity-rating reference so Venus lands the right tier. Decision flow = [[sop-library]] SOP-08. The governing policy = [[proof-vs-design-policy]].

> **Free-proof rule — RESOLVED 2026-06-22 (ruling):** Free proof is offered **only on big-ticket items priced $500 and above** (tents, displays, etc.). Items **under $500 — business cards, brochures, flyers — get NO free proof**, even at high quantity (e.g., 10,000 brochures), because design must be charged. The website already reflects this (not every product shows "Get a Free Proof"). So rule 6's "always close with free proof" applies to **$500+ big-ticket** quotes; for sub-$500 print, design is a paid line item. (source: Carlos direction 2026-06-22)

> **Venus's closing authority — RULED 2026-07-13 (Carlos, via Slack + the board meeting):**
> - **10% closer's margin — in-trade ONLY.** Venus may take up to **10% off** a sheet price on her own, but only in exchange for a commitment: **a deposit paid today, customer pickup instead of shipping, a Google review, or a referral.** Never as an opener; never stacked on the deposit unless it closes *today*. The price holds by default — the 10% buys the commitment. Deeper than 10%, or anything off-sheet, still routes to Carlos. *(Margin check: at the resolved ×2.0 high-ticket markup, 10% off an $800 tent trims gross margin from ~50% to ~44% — still above the 35% floor for $1K+ work; that's why it can't be stacked with a second concession. See [[pricing-engine]].)*
> - **Deposit-to-Lock — standard on every quote $800+.** Not a concession — a printed feature of the quote: **50% today locks the customer's price and a production slot for up to 60 days; the balance is due at proof approval, before print.** The standard answer to "it's more than I can do right now." **Threshold = $800+** (supersedes the earlier $900+ split-pay proposal in [[meta-funnel-review-2026-07-09]] rec #6). Mechanics: duplicate the draft → line item "50% Deposit — locks price + slot (60 days)" at half the total → send that invoice; on payment, tag the original draft `deposit-locked`, Meta stage → *converted*, balance invoice at proof approval. Full play in [[close-cadence-kit]] / [[deal-followup-playbook]]. (source: Carlos direction 2026-07-13 — posted to #promo-and-grow 8:15am, confirmed by Venus 9:03am.)

> **RULING 1 — Nonprofits pay sales tax. The absorb-it-inside-the-discount structure is RETIRED** (Carlos, 2026-09-19). California grants no nonprofit sales-tax exemption, and the workaround below — inflating the discount to swallow the tax so a promised out-the-door number held — cost real margin and was never required. **A nonprofit is now quoted, charged and remitted sales tax like any other California buyer.** The **8% nonprofit rate survives on its own**, for **organizations with a great cause** — Carlos's words, and deliberately a judgement call rather than a paperwork test. The bullet below is kept for the record because it explains how the two Compton invoices were built.

>
> **Free ground shipping ends.** Shipping is no longer bundled into the sheet price on any P&G order. Every quote from 9/28 forward either carries shipping as its own line or names pickup in Lynwood as the free alternative.
>
> **The 10% volume discount (RULING 2) ends.** It was created 2026-09-19 after Firm Footing and retired nine days later. It was applied once, on **Titan Law #D166 — $181.90** — and Titan converted on the nudge and the proof fix, not on the discount. The **10% in-trade closer's margin survives** (it buys a commitment) and the **8% nonprofit rate survives**. What is gone is the version that pays out simply because a customer asked.

The evidence from the same three weeks is that neither one closed a deal — Compton took past 20% and was lost on a spec gap; Titan took 10% and converted only after the artwork was fixed; Marisol never asked for either and paid full price.

> **RULING 2 — New volume discount: 10% off, on orders over $1,000 with multiple pieces, when the customer asks** (Carlos, 2026-09-19). **Multiple pieces means units, not distinct products** — three of the same tent qualifies. It came out of Firm Footing: Jerisse asked for bulk pricing on three tents, the first answer was "we don't run a quantity tier," and #D165 was revised to **$2,160 + $210.60 tax = $2,370.60** and re-sent 9/19.
>
> **Two tensions worth holding, both real.**
> (a) This sits against the standing **"never as an opener"** rule below, which exists precisely to stop an opening ask being rewarded. **Where they conflict on a $1,000+ multi-piece order, the new rule wins.** The in-trade 10% still governs everything under that threshold and every off-sheet ask.
> (b) **A discount that only exists when someone asks means the customer who trusts you pays the most.** In one week: Deborah asked twice and was conceded 16.9%; Jerisse asked once and got 10% on the second pass; Marisol never asked and paid full price on $1,055. If that spread is not intended, print the discount on qualifying quotes instead of waiting to be asked.
>
> **And the counter-evidence, from the same week: discounting did not close the deal.** Compton was conceded past 20% across two quotes — 8% plus absorbed tax on #D158, then a further 8% on a #D161 price that already carried 10% — **and the job was lost to another vendor anyway.** The likely cause was a spec gap, not price: she asked for an **18″ valance**, we offered **17″** and asked *her* to confirm she was comparing like for like against the other bids. → [[compton-high-alumni]]
>
> - **Sales tax is never waived — it is absorbed inside the discount.** *(HISTORICAL — superseded by Ruling 1 above, 2026-09-19.)* A 501(c)(3) is a **federal income-tax** exemption. California grants **no blanket sales-tax exemption for nonprofits** (CDTFA), so a promise to "exclude tax" cannot be honored as an exemption — the tax is still owed and still remitted. When tax has been promised verbally, hold the **out-the-door number** the customer was given and let the discount absorb the tax, so the state is paid and the customer's number is honored. Worked example (Compton High Alumni, 2026-09-10): two 10×10 custom canopies quoted at 8% off with tax "excluded" → the out-the-door total held at **$1,472**, with the **$142.88** of tax paid out of the discount rather than waived. **Never write "tax exempt" on a P&G order.**

> **Sales tax on a draft order reads $0 until the invoice is SENT — learned live 2026-09-16 (Evoria Health).** A draft with `taxExempt: false` and taxable line items still reports **$0.00 tax** while it sits as a draft, because Shopify has not resolved a tax jurisdiction yet. On `draftOrderInvoiceSend` it resolves and the total jumps. On #D164 that was **$1,055 → $1,168.42**, broken out as five real lines: California State 6%, LA County District 2.5%, LA County 0.25%, LA County Local 1%, **Lynwood City 1%** — **10.75%, resolved against the Lynwood origin**, i.e. the pickup situs. **Two rules follow.** (1) **Never quote a customer from a draft's own total** — it understates by the full tax. (2) **An unsent draft is not a price**; send it, or compute the tax yourself, before you put a number in an email. This is also why the #D161 fix on [[compton-high-alumni]] collapsed — replacing the line items dropped the discount with no computed tax to add back.

> **… and hand-computing the blended rate reads 1–2¢ light — learned live 2026-09-18 (Titan Law Firm #D166).** The workaround above ("compute the tax yourself before you put a number in an email") is right, but it does not land on the cent. Shopify rounds **each tax line independently**; a single blended multiplication rounds once. On #D166, $1,637.10 × 10.75% by hand = **$175.99**, while the five resolved lines summed to **$176.01** — a 2¢ undershoot, and it is systematic, not random: more lines and bigger subtotals widen it. So **quote the hand-computed figure as an estimate, never as the number** — say "estimated" in the email, and let the sent invoice be the authority. The five Lynwood-origin lines, for reference: CA State 6% · LA County District 2.5% · LA Co Local 1% · Lynwood City 1% · LA County 0.25%.

> **… and even a SENT invoice is only an estimate until a shipping address exists — proven 2026-09-20 (Evoria #D164 → order #P&G881080).** The invoice resolved against the **Lynwood origin at 10.75%** and read **$1,168.42**. She then checked out with a real **Pasadena 91107** address, the district lines **re-resolved to 10.50%**, and she paid **$1,165.78** — **$2.64 less**. Lynwood City 1% came off; Pasadena City 0.75% went on; state/county lines held. **So the full rule now reads: an unsent draft shows $0 (quote nothing from it) → a hand-computed blended rate is 1–2¢ light (label it an estimate) → a sent invoice is the origin-situs figure, and it moves again at checkout if the order ships somewhere else.** The practical consequence: **always tell the customer in writing that the final rate follows the delivery address**, and never promise an out-the-door number to the cent before you have that address. Direction is predictable — **shipping outside Lynwood lands at or below the invoice**, because the Lynwood city line is 1% and most LA-county cities are lower. That is a pleasant surprise, not a complaint, but a customer reconciling two documents still deserves the heads-up.

> **Drafts sent while unresolved are still sitting at $0 tax.** #D162 ($800) and #D163 ($1,855) to Evoria both went out INVOICE_SENT showing **$0.00 estimated taxes** and are **still live and payable**. If a customer pays one, the tax is collected from nobody and comes out of margin. **Standing cleanup rule: when a quote is superseded, void the old draft the same day.** Three payable invoices to one customer, two of them wrong, is an exposure, not a tidiness problem.

As the standalone SKU **`Custom 10ft Tent Back Wall (Full)`** it is **$320** — both reinforcement-strip variants. **The bundle is $150 dearer than buying the same wall separately**, so a customer who waits pays less than one who buys up front. Evoria was quoted **$320** for a later add-on because that is the number publicly listed on the store and she can find it. **Carlos to pick one.** The rest of the ladder for reference: Tent Only $800 · Half Wall $1,115 · Full Wall $1,270 · Complete Set $1,600; standalone half wall $240 single-sided / $400 double-sided. Complete Set less Full Wall = $330 for the two side half walls.

> **See also — referral-incentive design (flagged 2026-07-14):** Hormozi's lead-gen teaching ([[getting-customers-fast]] #9) argues incentives under ~50% don't change third-party behavior — so the **referral leg** of the 10%-in-trade margin is the weak one for building an *active* referral engine (it works fine as a live-close concession; it won't make partners hustle to send leads).
