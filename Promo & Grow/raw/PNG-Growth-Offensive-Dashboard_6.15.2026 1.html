1. Venus is the throttle, not the budget. At 5–10 leads/day while she's also closing reactivation + live deals, real capacity is ~3–6 quality conversations/day. Every lead past that line rots. The goal is max closed revenue per lead she can work — not max leads.

Act 1 — The Game Plan

Two engines, one funnel into Venus

Retargeting-first paid (highest-ROAS dollar) + a warmed cold-email machine — both throttled to what Venus can close.

Retargeting-first · feeds warm leads now

The "in the pocket" campaign · warms now, fires week 4

Act 2 — Red-Team

How an aggressive competitor beats this plan

"I run a bigger LA display shop with more budget and an SDR team. Here's where it cracks."

1

Your closer is the single point of failure. One closer at 5–10/day, already loaded. I don't beat your marketing — I let leads pile up unworked and reach them first.

2

You ceded high-intent Google Search. $0 to the person Googling "custom tents LA" right now. I own that auction; you fish where the fish are full.

3

Your wedge is copyable by Friday. "Free proof + 5-day" is a landing-page change for me. Once your ads prove it converts, I run the same Spanish creative with a bigger budget.

4

Cold email is a coin flip on a fragile base. One bad list and promotengrow.com is blacklisted. Send from the main domain once and your invoices die — replies already hit SPAM.

5

"This week" makes you sloppy. Rush cold before warmup → torched domain. Rush a lead form → junk leads. I bet you cut the warmup corner.

6

ROAS on a small budget is noise. A handful of closes/mo + one lumpy deal swings the number wildly. You'll cut a winner or fund a fluke.

7

Lead-form ads attract tire-kickers. Cheap CPL, terrible close rate. You celebrate "30 leads!" while cost-per-closed-deal explodes.

8

The Spanish-SMB niche is tight-knit. One slipped follow-through or late "5-day" delivery under new volume, and word poisons the well. I amplify any stumble.

9

No retention loop. All acquisition. You pay to win a $400 customer once; I build the reorder engine and win on LTV.

10

No kill-criteria or attribution discipline. "We'll watch ROAS" isn't a control system. By-memory attribution = you won't even know what worked.

Act 3 — The Hardened Rebuild

Same engines, fortified against every exposure

Each fix maps to the attack it neutralizes.

1 · Capacity is the design center
Neutralizes attacks 1, 7

Size inflow to ~5–6 quality leads/day. Start retargeting-only so first leads close fast. 60-sec qualifier filters tire-kickers. Auto-pause prospecting if backlog > 8 for 3 days — or green-light a 2nd closer.

2 · Add a ring-fenced Google Search line
Neutralizes attack 2

Carve $150/mo for 3–5 exact high-intent local terms, geo-fenced, negative-keyworded. Captures the ready-to-buy searcher. New split: $400 / $150 / $200.

3 · Protect the wedge with trust
Neutralizes attacks 3, 8

They can copy the words, not owned LA production + a bilingual human who calls in an hour + real relationships. Bundle the offer with logos, tenure ("formerly PrintCo Direct"), job photos, Spanish video testimonial.

4 · De-risk deliverability to near-zero
Neutralizes attacks 4, 5

Written rule: cold only from promotengrow.com. Option C (warmup + rotation + spam alarms). No corner-cut on warmup. Auto-pause at 0.08% spam. Separately fix the main-domain SPAM issue.

5 · Stable scoreboard, not noisy ROAS
Neutralizes attacks 6, 10

Judge on rolling 60-day cost-per-closed-deal (<$130) + leading indicators. Instrumented attribution (how'd-you-hear + UTM + HubSpot source). Written kill/scale rules.

6 · Bolt on the reorder loop
Neutralizes attack 9

Reactivation list = cheapest revenue; retarget past customers while Venus calls them (air + ground). Seasonal reorder triggers. Every new customer enters the loop day one.
