# Your First-Call Playbook
### You're reconnecting with people who already know us. Start a conversation — don't sell.

Venus — read this before you dial. The whole game on call one is simple: **get them to reply.** You're not closing, you're not pitching the catalog. You're a friendly voice from a shop they've already trusted, checking in. A conversation is **two-way** — until they say something back, it's just a one-way speech, and that's what gets people to hang up.

---

## The mindset (this is the big one)
You came from a call center where calls came *to* you. Making them flips the nerves — so flip the frame:
- **These aren't strangers. They bought from us before.** Warmest call there is.
- **Your only goal in the first 10–20 seconds: earn a reply.** One "oh yeah, hey" and you've won the opening. Closing comes much later.
- **You already have the gift.** You said it yourself — people can *hear your smile*, you build the relationship. That's the whole job. Trust it.
- **Be empathetic — they're busy (or acting busy) and didn't expect your call.** So be warm, deliberate, and short. Respect their time and they'll give you some.

---

## Step 1 — CONFIRM who you're talking to (don't skip this!)
Before any pitch: **make sure you've got the right person.**
> "Hi, is this **Alejandra**?"

Wait for the yes. *(This is the fix for the call that hung up — the spiel started before confirming, and a long intro to the wrong/unprepared person = click.)*

## Step 2 — A SHORT opener that earns a reply
Keep it to **2–3 sentences, max ~15 seconds.** Hit: who we are + who we *were* + the specific thing they ordered + a question. Then **stop talking.**

> "Hey Alejandra — this is **Venus** with **Promo & Grow**, you knew us as **PrintCo Direct**. Looks like about **7 months ago** you ordered a **banner stand and a tent** with us. **How's business going?**"

Why this works:
- **"Formerly PrintCo Direct"** instantly places us — "oh, *that* shop."
- **The specific past order + timeframe** (it's right on your call list — use it every time) proves we know them and jogs the memory: *"oh yeah, and I think we got business cards too…"* That's the door opening.
- **"How's business?"** hands them the mic.

**En español:**
> "Hola, ¿hablo con **Alejandra**? — Le habla **Venus** de **Promo & Grow**, antes **PrintCo Direct**. Hace como **7 meses** pidió un **banner y una carpa** con nosotros. ¿Cómo va el negocio?"

> The opener's only job is to get them talking — honestly, you could almost ask what color the sky is. **Any** question that invites a reply opens the door. Keep it short, make it about them.

## Step 3 — The second they reply, the conversation starts
Now keep it going by asking about **them** — not by pivoting to what we sell:
- "How's business been — anything new or exciting going on?"
- "How's growth this year? Busy season coming up?"
- "When you ordered that **[banner/tent]**, how'd it work out for you?"
- (When it's natural) even ask about **family / how they're doing** — real warmth, real relationship.

Get genuinely interested. People feel that — it's the whole reason they'll want to keep talking to you.

## Step 4 — Find the need *before* you offer anything
**Ask if they need anything right now** before you mention products. Be strategic about *when* to offer — only once the relationship's re-opened and you understand what they actually need. (Remember: someone says "brochure" but means a postcard; "a sign for my truck" might be a magnet or a wrap. Good questions get you to the *right* thing — that's what builds trust and sells.)

---

## What a good first call looks like (your real scoreboard)
✓ You confirmed the person. ✓ They **replied** — you got a real two-way exchange going. ✓ You learned one thing about their business. ✓ You set a next step (a proof, a follow-up text, or "I'll check back").
**That's a win — log it.** Not closing. Conversations started. The closes follow.

*You've got the hardest part already — the warmth. The rest is reps. Échale ganas.* 🎯
