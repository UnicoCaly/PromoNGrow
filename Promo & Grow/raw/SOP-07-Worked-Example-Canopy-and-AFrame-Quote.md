# SOP-07 — Worked Example: Quoting a Canopy + A-Frame Posters
### A real, start-to-finish quote build — with an AI-assisted discovery email.

**Recording:** [▶ Loom walkthrough](https://www.loom.com/share/c47333217e844198ab44a0216325237c) · **Owner:** Carlos · Added June 15, 2026

**What this shows:** how to turn an incoming pricing request into a clean Shopify draft-order estimate **and** a short, inquisitive quote email — including how to use ChatGPT/Claude to draft that email the right way. This is a *worked example* that puts SOP-02 (build the draft) and SOP-04 (the invoice/quote email) together on a real large-format job.

> ⚠️ **Note on the example client:** the request in this video came from an **institutional account (City of South Gate)**, which is **Carlos-only / DNC for Venus**. ## The scenario
An existing customer messaged requesting pricing on a **10×10 canopy** and **10 A-frame posters**. We'd done A-frame posters for them before (standard 24×36). They didn't specify canopy options.

## Step 1 — Start with the headline item (the canopy)
- The **10×10 canopy** is the top/anchor item — build it first.
- Since they **didn't specify** a configuration, **quote the standard, most basic option** — then *in the email* let them know we offer other configurations and can bundle.
- Options chosen in the example: **white reinforcement + standard background.**
- Example price: **$800 for the standard 10×10 canopy.** *(Always confirm against the current Master Price Sheet — walls/print configs change the price; full/half-wall builds are priced separately.)*

## Step 2 — Add the A-frame posters
- A-frame **PVC signboards** are on the website as a catalog item — add them to the draft.
- Spec in the example: **PVC (8 mm), 24×36.**
- **Quantity: 10.** Price in the example: **$50 each.**
- **Confirm the size with the client.** Here we didn't assume — we ask them to confirm 24×36 in the email.

## Step 3 — Attach the client
- The customer was **already in the system** (returning client) — search and attach, don't re-create. *(See SOP-03.)*
- No phone on file → we email. *(If a phone exists, a quick call beats email — see the First-Call Playbook.)*

## Step 4 — Draft the quote email (the important part)
Use AI (ChatGPT/Claude) to draft, but **steer it** — the goal isn't just to send a price, it's a **short, inquisitive email that uncovers their real needs and timeline.** We offer many tent configurations, so we ask before assuming.

**What we fed the AI (the recipe):**
- The request: 10×10 canopy + 10× 24×36 A-frame posters.
- The prices we're quoting ($800 canopy / $50 each poster).
- The ask: *"Help me draft a short, inquisitive email to understand the client's needs and timeline — we offer a lot of tent configurations and want to set them up with what they actually need."*
- Include: a nudge to **check out our site** for configurations.

**The lesson from the video:** the first AI draft came back **too long and wordy.** Fix = tell it to **"keep it brief and concise,"** then read it over and trim. A long quote email buries the question; a short one gets a reply. *(Same principle as the Follow-Up Email Playbook: personal, specific, one clear ask.)*

## Step 5 — Send
- Confirm the estimate is saved and the client attached.
- Send the email from the rep's own email (CC `hello@promongrow.com`), per SOP-04.

---

## The reusable takeaways (for future builds)
1. **Anchor item first**, then add-ons.
2. **Unspecified config → quote the standard**, mention upgrades/bundles in the email.
3. **Confirm sizes/specs** rather than assuming.
4. **Returning client → find, don't duplicate.**
5. **AI drafts the email, but you steer it**: feed it the specs + prices, ask for **short + inquisitive** (needs + timeline), include a site link, then trim.
6. **Price is the start of a conversation, not the end** — the discovery question is what moves the deal.
