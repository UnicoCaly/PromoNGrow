## 1. THE ONE-LINE TRUTH

**Promo & Grow equips the entrepreneur operating from *ganas* to show up like a pro — fast.**

We don't sell tents and banners. We sell the moment a scrappy hustle starts looking legit.
Everything — the 5-day speed, the free proof, the bilingual warmth — is proof of one belief:
**we're in your corner, and we won't let your drive go unsupported.**

## 2. THE STORY (why this brand exists)

Promo & Grow is the evolution of **PrintCo Direct Inc.**, built on its founding promise — *"Promote & Grow."*
For nearly two decades, founder Carlos and his team have helped businesses stand out through high-impact
visual branding. PrintCo installed a full design team to bring client visions to life. **Promo & Grow
carries that same foresight forward — but with a sharper belief: empower the entrepreneur to do it
themselves, with our support.**

P&G is the answer to the **do-it-yourselfer operating from *ganas*** — the raised-on-hustle entrepreneur
taking on their own marketing and branding. Some are doing it right; others are working hard at it and
need backup. P&G meets both:

- **For the one with the design + technical foundation:** *"We'll just print for ya."* Done right,
  delivered in 5 business days.
- **For the one who needs help:** we provide the resources and support to empower them to do it —
  so they show up like pros.

**One belief, two doors.** Both serve the same person — the entrepreneur with more heart than
resources — at different stages of their journey. *(Ganas = the hunger, the drive, the will to push
when you've got more ambition than budget. It's the fuel of the self-made, and it's who this brand
is for.)*

## 4. WHO WE'RE FOR (personas — designed from the heart outward)

**Design the brand's heart for Persona 1, and let it scale up to 2 & 3.** A brand that authentically
champions the underdog can still win the corporate buyer; a brand built for corporate cannot
authentically serve the underdog. Build from the heart out.

### 🎯 Persona 1 — "La Emprendedora" (THE HEART OF THE BRAND)
The local Spanish-speaking small-business owner — taquería, party/quinceañera vendor, panadería,
food truck, neighborhood shop. Operates on *ganas*. Has a festival, grand opening, or swap-meet booth
coming and needs to look legit — fast. Lives on her phone, prefers Spanish, trusts people over
platforms. **Fear:** looking small/amateur at her moment. **Dream:** showing up looking like she's
already made it. **What wins her:** warmth, speed, a real person who speaks her language, a free proof
that removes risk.

### 📈 Persona 2 — "The Brand Manager" (Donut Media type)
Marketing/brand manager at a growing company. Has a trade show or activation, often on a tight
deadline. Knows design, may have their own assets. **Fear:** missing the deadline / vendor flaking.
**Dream:** a partner who just *delivers*, fast, no drama. **What wins them:** speed, reliability,
"send us your files, we'll print it right in 5 days," proven track record.

### 🎪 Persona 3 — "The Agency / Event Pro"
Runs experiential activations and events for clients. Needs a dependable production partner who makes
*them* look good. **Fear:** quality misses that reflect on them. **Dream:** a go-to vendor they can
trust repeatedly. **What wins them:** consistency, craft, speed, easy reorders.

**The unifying truth across all three:** *someone with a moment that can't wait, something to prove,
and the drive to show up bigger than they are.* Same human heart, three income levels.

## 6. VOICE & TONE

**The voice in one line:** A bilingual growth partner — warm, confident, encouraging, and real.
Talks *with* you like a neighbor who happens to be a pro, never *at* you like a vendor.

**Chosen register (locked):** *"Tu evento, tu marca, hecho bien. Send your logo, get a free proof,
booth-ready in 5 days. Easy."* — bilingual, community, easy, reassuring.

### Voice attributes
1. **Encouraging** — we believe in the customer's hustle; we hype the dream, not the product.
2. **Direct & clear** — plain talk, no jargon, no fluff. Specific (the "5 days," the "free proof").
3. **Warm & human** — there's a real person here who cares about your event going well.
4. **Confident, never boastful** — confidence comes from delivering (we hit 3 days for Donut), not bragging.
5. **Bilingual by nature** — natural code-switching the way real bilingual Angelenos talk.

### ⚠️ The bilingual craft rule (critical)
Spanglish only works with taste. Use natural code-switching the way bilingual LA actually speaks —
a Spanish phrase that carries warmth (*"hecho bien," "con ganas," "tu marca"*) dropped into clear
English, or fully parallel ES/EN where needed. **Never** machine-translated, never gimmicky, never
forced. Done right, it reads as confident and modern to *everyone*, including the corporate buyer.
Done lazily, it reads as a gimmick and cheapens the brand. When in doubt, read it aloud — if a real
bilingual person wouldn't say it that way, rewrite it.

### THE ANTI-BRAND — what P&G must NEVER be
- ❌ **Never salesy or pushy** — we equip, we don't pressure. The free proof is the opposite of a hard sell.
- ❌ **Never cold or corporate** — no faceless print-vendor energy. A real person stands behind this.
- ❌ **Never condescending** — the ganas entrepreneur is a hero, not someone who "can't afford better."
  Respect the hustle. Never talk down to it.
- ❌ **Never cheap-looking** — accessible ≠ bargain-bin. The work and the brand must look *premium* and
  *legit*, even though they're attainable. (This is the #1 visual guardrail — see §8.)
- ❌ **Never generic** — no stock-photo, templated, "any print shop" vibe. We're specifically for the
  ganas entrepreneur, and it should feel that way.
- ❌ **Never overpromise** — we say 5 days and hit 5 days. Confidence through delivery, not hype.
- ❌ **Never unenthusiastic** — we're genuinely excited about the customer's project and their win.

### Voice in action (examples)
- **Hero line:** "Tu evento, tu marca, hecho bien." / "Your event. Your brand. Done right."
- **Speed:** "Booth-ready in 5 business days. Guaranteed." (not "fast turnaround available")
- **Proof:** "Free proof before you pay — nothing prints without your approval." (reassurance, not sales)
- **Encouragement:** "You bring the ganas. We'll make sure you show up like a pro."
- **CTA:** "Send your logo →" / "Get your free proof →" (low-friction, conversational — never "BUY NOW")

### B. What we need from the engagement
**1. Brand foundation (identity):**
- Logo system (primary, secondary, mark/icon, clear-space, sizing)
- Color palette with primary/secondary + print AND web/digital breakdowns (you offer this — great)
- Typography system (display, headline, body, UI) with hierarchy + bilingual character support
- Logo usage do's & don'ts, iconography style, photography/imagery direction
- The brand's look must read: **established, premium, energetic, warm, trustworthy** — for the
  entrepreneur who wants to look like a market leader.

**2. Digital design system (Figma — the part you confirmed you do):**
This is the bridge Carlos wants to build (he comes from print; the digital system is the gap).
We need a real, usable system — not a static guide that calls itself Figma:
- **Design tokens / variables:** colors, type, spacing as reusable variables (one source of truth)
- **Spacing & layout:** an 8-pt grid, padding/margin scale, breakpoints (mobile-first — our buyers
  are on phones)
- **Components with states:** buttons (default/hover/pressed/disabled), forms & inputs, cards, nav,
  badges, the quote/"free proof" CTA, trust elements — all with real interaction states
- **Accessibility:** contrast ratios that pass, touch-target sizes
- **Usage docs** so Carlos + Claude Code + future hires can build on-brand without you in the room
- **End goal:** this system becomes the toolkit for the P&G website (which must eventually convert
  on its own) AND a reusable asset for future client work / the Proágo side.

### C. Application priorities (where the brand shows up first)
1. The website / digital storefront (PDPs, the quote/free-proof flow, trust elements)
2. Social content templates (Instagram/Facebook — for the marketing engine)
3. Email template system (on-brand campaign templates)
4. Bilingual everything — the system must work in EN and ES without breaking

### D. The vibe check (read these before you design)
- It should feel like: **the trusted pro who's genuinely rooting for you** — warm, sharp, dependable,
  a little bold, unmistakably legit.
- It should NOT feel like: a discount print site, a cold corporate vendor, a generic template, or
  a sleepy local sign shop.
- The test for any asset: *Would this make a taquería owner feel proud to show up with it — AND make
  a Donut Media brand manager trust us with their deadline?* If yes, it's on-brand.
